About

Hey, I'm Ibsa.

My favorite moment in engineering is the first time something I built actually turns on. An LED blinks, a motor spins, a ball bounces across a screen on an FPGA I wrote in Verilog. I chase that feeling a lot.

These days it's Project Artemis at Makers USC, a device that helps you aim a bow and arrow by giving you visual or audio cues. We're still deciding which works better. Before that I wrote firmware and designed the board for Hydro, a little robot that brings snacks and water to your airplane seat. I also spent a year building AI demos with Google and USC PhD researchers, including an LED setup you can boss around with Siri.

I've also soldered my own guitar circuit (it works, and it's loud), and spent 36 sleepless hours at a hackathon building a wearable that tells blind users what's in front of them. I'd love to keep building things that help people.

I grew up in Takoma Park, Maryland, and my family is originally from Jimma, Ethiopia. When I'm not at a workbench, I'm playing soccer with friends or hunting down a food I haven't tried yet. Send me your favorite spot.

I do all this while working about 15 hours a week, so if you see me on campus, I'm probably walking fast.
